# Sopita user guide

Sopita is a lightweight audio trimmer, slicer, and effects processor. It is intended for preparing samples easily for hardware samplers and tracker software. Here is a quick rundown on how to use it.

This app was made by [Sergio Rodríguez Gómez](https://srsergiorodriguez.github.io/). If you want to support its development, please consider donating on [itch](https://srsergior.itch.io/sopita).

## Loading audio

When you open the app, you will see the splash screen. Just click the button to browse for a WAV or MP3 file on your computer. Once loaded, the waveform will appear in the main workspace.

## Editing modes

You have two main ways to work with your audio. You can toggle between them in the toolbar. In any of the modes you can press the play button or the spacebar to play the selected sample, or press the double play button or shift + space to play the whole loaded audio file.

### Trim mode

Use trim mode when you want to extract a single loop or sample from the file. You can drag the left and right edges of the highlighted region on the waveform to set your start and end points. You can also use the left and right arrow keys to nudge the start point by a single millisecond. Hold the shift key and use the arrow keys to nudge the end point.

### Slice mode and transient detection

Slice mode is for chopping up a drum break or a full track into multiple smaller hits. When you switch to slice mode, the app automatically places markers based on sudden volume spikes in the audio. 

You can adjust how many slices are generated by turning the sensitivity knob. Higher sensitivity catches quieter sounds and subtle details. Lower sensitivity only triggers on the loudest peaks. Click on any slice to make it active. You can remove slices by double clicking them.

## Envelopes and fades

To prevent your samples from clicking abruptly at the beginning or end, you can apply quick volume fades. Use the fade in and fade out knobs located in the toolbar. These values are measured in milliseconds. The waveform will visually update to show you the exact curve of the fade being applied to your active regions.

## The effects rack

At the bottom of the screen is your signal processing chain. You can turn individual modules on and off using the power button under each knob. The main knob controls the intensity of the effect.

You can change the signal flow by clicking the left and right arrows next to the power button to move a module around in the chain. The audio signal always travels from left to right. There is also a master volume knob on the far right to control the final output level and prevent digital clipping.

Note: a known quirk, if you apply the decimator effect and then export with the lofi preset, the succesive qulaity reduction might produce weird sound artifacts.

## Exporting

When your audio sounds right, click the export button in the top right corner. 

If you are in trim mode, it will process and export your single sample. If you are in slice mode, you can choose to export all your slices as separate individual files, or render them into a single continuous grid file with equal spacing (this is useful for hardware samplers that use that equal spacing for mapping samples in a keyboard, like the Sonicware Liven Lofi-12). The exported files will have all your fades and effects permanently burned into the audio.
